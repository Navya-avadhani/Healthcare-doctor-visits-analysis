# Healthcare Analytics for Doctor Visits

## Project Overview

This project analyzes factors associated with the number of doctor visits using demographic, health, illness, chronic-condition, and insurance-related variables.

The analysis uses Python-based exploratory data analysis and visualization to identify patterns in healthcare utilization.

## Problem Statement

Healthcare providers and administrators need to understand the factors associated with doctor visits to better understand healthcare utilization patterns.

This project examines how health conditions, illness, chronic conditions, demographic characteristics, income, and insurance-related factors are associated with the number of doctor visits.

## Dataset

* **Records:** 5,190 patient records
* **Variables:** 12 analysis variables
* **Target variable:** Number of doctor visits
* **Key variables:** Gender, age, income, illness, reduced activity, health condition, insurance coverage, and chronic conditions

## Objectives

* Analyze the distribution of doctor visits.
* Examine relationships between health-related factors and doctor visits.
* Compare doctor visits across demographic and income groups.
* Analyze the association of chronic conditions with doctor visits.
* Examine patterns across insurance and healthcare-access categories.
* Identify factors showing stronger associations with healthcare utilization.

## Key Findings

* Most respondents recorded zero doctor visits, while higher visit counts were considerably less frequent.
* Average doctor visits generally increased with the number of reported illnesses.
* Doctor visits showed an upward pattern across higher recorded health-condition levels.
* Respondents with a recorded long-term chronic condition had higher average doctor visits than those without one.
* Health-related variables showed stronger associations with doctor visits than demographic or income-related variables.
* Female respondents recorded a higher average number of doctor visits than male respondents.
* Doctor visits varied across income groups, but no consistent upward or downward pattern was observed.
* Doctor visit patterns varied across the recorded insurance and healthcare-access categories.

## Business / Healthcare Implications

The analysis suggests that healthcare utilization is more closely associated with health-related characteristics than income alone. Understanding patterns related to illness, health conditions, reduced activity, and chronic conditions can help healthcare organizations better understand patient utilization patterns.

The findings can be relevant to:

* Hospitals and healthcare providers
* Healthcare administrators
* Health insurance organizations
* Public health organizations
* Healthcare analysts and researchers
* Healthcare planning teams

## Limitations

* The analysis identifies associations and patterns but does not establish causation.
* The dataset represents recorded patient characteristics and doctor visits and may not capture all factors affecting healthcare utilization.
* The analysis is based on the variables available in the dataset.
